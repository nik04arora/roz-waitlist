## Can you start an SIP with ₹500 in India?

Yes, you can start an SIP with ₹500 when the mutual fund scheme accepts a ₹500 instalment. Some schemes accept smaller amounts and some require more, so always check the current scheme document before setting up the instruction.

An SIP, or Systematic Investment Plan, is a way to invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals. The ₹500 is the instalment amount, not a separate type of investment. The mutual fund scheme you select determines where the money is invested and how much market risk you take.

If SIP mechanics are new to you, begin with [what an SIP means and how it works](/learn/what-is-an-sip/).

## Is ₹500 the minimum SIP amount for every mutual fund?

No, India does not have one universal minimum SIP amount for every mutual fund scheme. The minimum can depend on the scheme, frequency, platform route and the number of instalments you agree to register.

Current official documents show how much the terms can differ:

| Example from an official document | SIP frequency | Minimum shown | Minimum instalments |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Unifi Flexi Cap Fund standard SIP | Weekly or monthly | ₹500 | 6 |
| Unifi Flexi Cap Fund Choti SIP | Monthly | ₹250 | 60 |
| LIC Mutual Fund general scheme terms | Daily | ₹100 | 60 |
| LIC Mutual Fund general scheme terms | Weekly | ₹150 | 40 |
| LIC Mutual Fund general scheme terms | Monthly | ₹200 | 30 |

The [Unifi Flexi Cap Fund Scheme Information Document](https://portal.amfiindia.com/spages/14203.pdf), accessed 11 September 2026, lists a ₹500 minimum for its standard weekly and monthly SIP and ₹250 for its eligible Choti SIP. The [LIC Mutual Fund Statement of Additional Information](https://portal.amfiindia.com/spages/sai18.pdf), accessed the same day, shows different minimums for different SIP frequencies and asks investors to check the relevant scheme document.

These examples explain the variation. They are not suggestions to invest in either fund. Minimum amounts and facility rules can change, so verify the latest Scheme Information Document, or SID, before acting.

## What is a ₹250 Choti SIP?

A Choti SIP is a small-ticket SIP facility created for eligible investors who are new to the mutual fund industry. It is a specific framework with a ₹250 instalment and additional conditions, not a new mutual fund category or the minimum for every SIP.

SEBI's [policy record updated through 20 March 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/mar-2026/Policy%20related%20Letters%20and%20Emails%20issued%20by%20SEBI%20as%20on%20March%2020%202026_p.pdf), accessed 11 September 2026, records the framework issued on 20 February 2025. Its main conditions include:

- the facility is intended for investors who are new to the mutual fund industry;
- the special small-ticket treatment is restricted to three ₹250 SIPs, with one in each of up to three asset management companies;
- it is available only in the Growth option of eligible schemes;
- debt, sectoral, thematic, small-cap and mid-cap schemes are excluded from the framework;
- payment is through NACH or UPI AutoPay;
- the registration carries a commitment of 60 investments; and
- an investor is still allowed to stop the SIP or withdraw early, subject to the scheme's normal exit-load terms.

The eligible scheme list and operational terms can change. Confirm that the selected scheme currently offers Choti SIP and that you meet the stated eligibility conditions.

## Is a ₹500 SIP worth starting?

A ₹500 SIP can be useful when it is an affordable way to begin investing and learn how mutual funds behave. It will not produce a large corpus quickly on its own, and starting with a small amount does not guarantee a profit.

The value comes from three practical things: beginning with an amount you can sustain, learning to read your statements and seeing how the chosen fund moves in real markets. You can increase the amount later if your income and savings capacity rise.

Do not invest money needed soon merely to build a habit. Rent, fees, debt payments and a suitable emergency buffer should not depend on an equity fund being up when you need the money.

## How much do small SIP contributions add up to?

The arithmetic is simple when returns are left out. A ₹500 monthly SIP contributes ₹6,000 in one year and ₹30,000 in five years, assuming every instalment is processed and the amount never changes.

| Monthly amount | Contributions in 1 year | Contributions in 5 years |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ₹250 | ₹3,000 | ₹15,000 |
| ₹500 | ₹6,000 | ₹30,000 |
| ₹1,000 | ₹12,000 | ₹60,000 |

These are contribution totals, not future investment values. The actual value can be higher or lower because mutual fund returns are market-linked, and costs or taxes may apply when relevant.

Large return projections can make a small SIP look more certain than it is. A calculator result depends on the assumed return, investment period and whether the SIP increases. Treat it as an illustration, never as a forecast.

## Does a smaller SIP mean lower investment risk?

A smaller SIP reduces the rupee amount exposed through each instalment, but it does not change the risk level of the chosen fund. A ₹500 SIP in a very-high-risk equity scheme still buys units of that very-high-risk scheme.

Roz Invest's two-size check separates these decisions:

| Size to check | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Instalment size | Can you afford this amount every month without using money needed soon? |
| Investment-risk size | Can the chosen mutual fund fall more than you can financially and emotionally tolerate? |

Passing the first check does not automatically pass the second. Read the scheme's Riskometer, objective, portfolio, time horizon and exit-load terms before investing. If your ability to bear losses is unclear, consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser for personalised advice.

## What do you need before starting a ₹500 SIP?

You need completed Know Your Customer, or KYC, requirements, an eligible investment route, a supported bank account and a scheme that accepts the amount. A smaller SIP does not remove the normal onboarding or verification steps.

AMFI's [KYC guidance](https://www.amfiindia.com/investor/become-mf-distributor?zoneName=KnowYourCustomer), accessed 11 September 2026, states that KYC is mandatory irrespective of the investment amount and specifically applies that rule to a ₹500 SIP. AMFI's [guide to investing in mutual funds](https://www.amfiindia.com/investor/become-mf-distributor?zoneName=HowtoInvestInMF), accessed the same day, explains that an investor can use an AMC directly, an AMFI-registered mutual fund distributor or other supported transaction routes after meeting KYC requirements.

Before registering, check:

1. your KYC status and the details required by the chosen route;
2. the bank account that will fund the instalments;
3. the exact scheme, plan and option;
4. the SIP amount, frequency, date and minimum number of instalments;
5. the scheme Riskometer and investment objective; and
6. the cancellation, pause, exit-load and redemption terms.

## Can you start an SIP without a salary, PAN or demat account?

You do not need a regular salary or a demat account for every SIP route, but you must satisfy KYC and the chosen scheme's requirements. PAN is normally part of mutual fund onboarding, although a limited PEKRN-based route exists for eligible small-ticket investments under prescribed conditions.

AMFI's KYC page says investors provide an income slab and occupation as additional information, but no proof of income is required. That means being a student or having irregular income does not by itself prevent an eligible person from investing. The money should still be legally yours to invest and should not be required for near-term expenses.

For PAN, do not interpret a small SIP as permission to skip KYC. SEBI's Choti SIP framework permits eligible investment of up to ₹50,000 per investor, per mutual fund, per financial year without PAN through a PAN Exempt KYC Registration Number, or PEKRN. It also requires Aadhaar for a new PEKRN when PAN is not provided. Check the current eligibility and documentation with the AMC or platform.

For holding mode, mutual funds can be held in Statement of Account, or SOA, form as well as demat form. The Choti SIP framework permits both. The article on [moving mutual funds between apps](/learn/transfer-mutual-funds-from-one-app-to-another/) explains why the holding mode matters later.

## Can you increase a ₹500 SIP later?

Often, yes, but the available method depends on the scheme and platform. You may be able to edit the amount, add a step-up instruction or register a separate SIP, while a Choti SIP can have different conditions.

Do not assume that changing the amount is automatic. Check whether the bank mandate is large enough, whether a new registration is required and when the new amount will begin. The detailed guide to [increasing or reducing an SIP amount](/learn/can-you-change-sip-amount/) explains these routes.

A future increase should follow your actual cash flow. The guide to [calculating how much of your salary can go into SIPs](/learn/how-much-salary-invest-in-sip/) starts with take-home pay, essential expenses and near-term obligations rather than a fixed percentage.

## What should a beginner check before choosing a small SIP?

Check the investment before celebrating the low minimum. A small entry amount can make a scheme accessible, but it does not tell you whether the fund fits your goal, time horizon or ability to take risk.

Ask these questions:

- What is the money for, and when might I need it?
- What does the scheme invest in?
- What risk level does its current Riskometer show?
- Is the plan Direct or Regular, and is the option Growth or IDCW?
- What are the current expense ratio and exit-load terms?
- Does the ₹500 minimum apply to this exact scheme and frequency?
- How many instalments are required during registration?
- Can I continue the amount during months when expenses rise?

The fund name with the highest recent return is not automatically the right starting point. If your available amount later grows to ₹10,000, use the separate guide to [planning a ₹10,000 monthly mutual fund investment](/learn/how-to-invest-10000-per-month-in-mutual-funds/) rather than simply adding more schemes.

## What is the practical rule for starting with ₹500?

Start with ₹500 only when the scheme permits it, the amount is affordable and you understand the fund's risk. Verify the exact minimum and terms in the current scheme document because the app's headline amount may not explain every condition.

A small SIP can be a sensible learning amount. Keep the expectation equally grounded: the first success is making an informed, sustainable investment, not reaching a projected corpus on a calculator.
